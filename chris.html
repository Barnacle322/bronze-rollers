Basic Stacking Principles

Good stacking isn't just about clearing lines. It's about keeping your board manageable and leaving yourself options for the pieces still to come. Learning to stack cleanly makes everything else easier.

Level 2 of 4

On this page

Keep it Flat

Aim for a balanced surface without tall spikes, deep gaps, or awkward overhangs. A completely flat board isn't always ideal; small steps give S, Z, J, and L pieces places to fit. Before placing a piece, consider what shapes you'll leave behind and whether your upcoming pieces can fill them.

Leave one well

A simple strategy called 9-0 stacking leaves one column open at the edge of the board. Build across the other nine columns, then use an I-piece to clear four lines at once. Keep the well accessible, but don't sacrifice the rest of your stack just to preserve it. If the board becomes too high, prioritize clearing lines safely.

Dealing with holes

A hole is an empty cell trapped beneath another block. Holes restrict your placements and usually require clearing the rows above them to recover. Check where each piece will land before using hard drop, and avoid burying existing holes under more blocks.

  • Look at the next queue and plan a few pieces ahead.
  • Use hold to manage awkward pieces, not just to save I-pieces
  • Prefer placemenets that leave several options for your next move
Try this

Play a 40-line sprint without worrying about speed. Focus on keeping your stack manageable, avoiding covered holes, and checking the next queue before each placement. Aim to finish with zero holes.
